Why intermediaries break

Every layer between the funder and the farmer is a place money can stall or leak.

Lending through middlemen

TaniFund lent through intermediaries, ended with poor repayment and lost its OJK licence. Goldfinch lost about $18M to emerging-market lending partners.

Lists change after the fact

Aid fraud cases in Indonesia keep pointing at the same weak spots: who is on the list, and who actually got paid.

Our own first version

NusaHarvest V1 routed funds through cooperatives. No real money ever moved, and its 40mm/30-day trigger would have fired in 23 of 25 years.

Lesson: remove the money intermediary. Extension workers can help farmers register, but they never hold funds.

Sources: DL News, Goldfinch defaults · Antara, KPK bansos case · V1 figure: research/data/backtest-*.csv
The idea

A conditional grant: sponsors lock their own funds, and a public rainfall rule decides.

Locked before

A sponsor (CSR team, zakat institution, local government) locks its own funds in a Solana escrow before October.

Sealed roster

The farmer list is sealed as a merkle root before the season. Nobody can be added after a drought is visible.

Public trigger

If Oct-Dec satellite rainfall falls below the district floor (its own 20th percentile), the grant is paid after a 48-hour dispute window.

Farmers pay nothing, ever, and never touch crypto. A licensed payment provider sends rupiah to their e-wallet. NusaHarvest never holds funds; Solana is the public ledger and the sponsor's escrow.

How it works

Four steps, each one checkable by anyone.

01 LOCK

Fund the vault

Sponsor deposits USDC into a campaign account with district, window and threshold fixed.

02 SEAL

Freeze the roster

Registrations are hashed into a merkle root and written on-chain at freeze time.

03 SETTLE

Post the rainfall

After Dec 31 the operator posts observed ERA5 millimetres plus a hash of the source data.

04 RELEASE

Disburse

An auditor has 48 hours to dispute. After that, release sends funds to the payment partner, who pays rupiah to e-wallets.

Why Solana

The chain does three jobs a spreadsheet cannot.

Money can't back out

Funds sit in a program vault before the season. A sponsor cannot quietly withdraw once a drought is on the news.

List can't grow

The roster root is written before freeze. Any farmer can check their own inclusion in the browser against the on-chain root.

Result can be replayed

Observed rainfall and a data hash sit on-chain. Anyone can rerun the public script during the dispute window.

Base fee per settle or roster transaction is 0.000005 SOL, so a campaign's on-chain cost is noise next to a Rp2,500 bank transfer fee. USDC on Solana is native and widely supported by licensed payment partners.

Business model

Sponsors pay for an accountable grant, not for yield.

Who pays

  • CSR teams that need audit-ready reports
  • Zakat institutions (national ZIS collection 2025: Rp44.7T)
  • Local governments, NGOs, rice mills and feed offtakers

How we earn

  • Platform fee of 3-5% of locked campaign funds (planned, not yet validated with a sponsor)
  • No interest, no token, farmers pay nothing
  • Unused funds roll to next season or return to the sponsor

Worked example, assumption only: expected sponsor cost about Rp60,000 per farmer per year plus a Rp2,500 disbursement fee. It sits alongside AUTP as a top-up, not in place of it. Funds move through a licensed payment partner; NusaHarvest never holds them.
